## Figure 1. Leakage-controlled transition forecasting and the PK-SSM representation

**a**, Each origin uses 300 s of causal heart-rate and acceleration context to forecast every second of the subsequent 120-s trajectory. **b**, Five participant-disjoint folds separate training, validation, calibration, and sealed testing; each of 31 participants is assigned to test exactly once. **c**, PK-SSM uses a causal temporal encoder, a normalized future-workload trajectory, bounded fast and slow first-order states with separate rise and recovery constants, a bounded neural residual, and a heteroscedastic Student-t output followed by source-only conformal calibration. **d**, The evidence boundaries distinguish held-out users, current-context conditioning, independent GoldenCheetah refitting, and the completed bidirectional activity-shift evaluation. Physiologically named states are model-internal latent variables rather than physiological measurements.

## Figure 2. Held-out-user accuracy does not support PK-SSM superiority

**a**, Five-seed arithmetic-ensemble mean absolute error (MAE) at 30, 60, and 120 s on tagged transitions from 31 held-out participants. **b**, Participant-level paired PK-SSM minus TCN differences with 95% confidence intervals from 10,000 participant bootstrap resamples. Positive values favor TCN. Wilcoxon signed-rank tests were sensitivity analyses and are reported in the source-data table. **c**, Complete-trajectory MAE for tagged transitions and fixed-stride schedule-wide origins. **d**, Fixed-seed baseline ranking under the same participant roles and causal inputs. Raw Ridge extrapolation is displayed as an outside-axis value. Panels **a-c** use five-seed ensembles; panel **d** uses the canonical seed and must not be interpreted as an ensemble ranking.

## Figure 3. Dynamic-fidelity and uncertainty limitations

**a**, De-identified examples selected algorithmically at the 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles of the mean per-origin PK-SSM and TCN error. Selection used no participant, protocol, or event label. **b**, Predicted-to-observed total-variation and rapid-change amplitude ratios; a ratio of one is ideal. **c**, Origin-level conformal marginal coverage at 30, 60, and 120 s, with the nominal 0.95 target shown by a dashed line. **d**, Mean full prediction-interval width under origin-level and participant-block calibration. **e**, MAE in high-heart-rate targets defined by fold-specific training/calibration 90th percentiles and by a fixed 160-beats-per-minute sensitivity threshold. PK-SSM was markedly over-smoothed, TCN accumulated excessive variation, and neither model reproduced rapid changes well.

## Figure 4. Independent GoldenCheetah replication changes model ranking

**a**, Participant-macro tagged-transition MAE after every architecture was independently refitted on GoldenCheetah training sessions. **b**, Participant-bootstrap PK-SSM minus TCN differences for trajectory and horizon-specific endpoints; confidence intervals crossed zero for the primary trajectory comparison. **c**, Descriptive within-dataset strata with participant counts; solid lines show tagged transitions and dashed lines show schedule-wide origins. All displayed activities or sports appeared during fitting. **d**, Total-variation ratios and source-calibrated 120-s interval widths in the Wearable and GoldenCheetah datasets. GoldenCheetah is an independent dataset-specific replication, not zero-shot transfer or an unseen-sport experiment.

## Figure 5. Kinetic parameters are not stable physiological traits

**a**, Spearman associations of the `rest_hr` and `hr_reserve` latent variables with current and future mean heart rate. **b**, Participant intraclass correlation plotted against mean pairwise rank agreement across five random seeds. The four rise and recovery time constants are grouped under one callout for readability; each remains a separate point. The shaded upper-right region denotes simultaneous participant separation and seed agreement. **c**, Median within-origin standard deviation across seeds for fast and slow rise and recovery constants. **d**, Fraction of origins near either finite hard bound. The audit shows an identifiability and initialization-stability limitation rather than simple hard-bound clipping. All parameters are context-conditioned model variables and must not be interpreted as biomarkers, physiological measurements, or stable phenotypes.

## Figure 6. Strict bidirectional activity-shift evaluation

**a**, Participant-macro trajectory MAE under the primary tagged-events policy for the source-user, seen-user/new-activity, and joint-user-and-activity boundaries. Models were trained on the protocol group named in parentheses, and activity identity was masked from all model inputs. **b**, Participant-paired PK-SSM minus TCN trajectory-MAE differences at the joint boundary under the primary tagged-events and secondary evaluation-stride policies. Negative values favor PK-SSM. **c**, Paired activity-shift deployment contrasts, defined as joint-user-and-activity MAE minus source-user MAE. Negative values indicate that the joint target was easier than the source-user boundary; positive values indicate that it was harder. This contrast is not a causal estimate of activity shift because source and target protocol groups may differ in intrinsic difficulty. **d**, Fold-macro 120-s participant-block conformal coverage versus mean interval width at the primary joint boundary. The dashed line marks nominal 0.95 coverage. Forest-plot points show participant-paired mean differences and bars show 95% confidence intervals from 10,000 participant-bootstrap replicates. Results pool five locked seeds within each of five outer folds; sample sizes were 29-31 participants depending on boundary. Source data are provided with the figure.
